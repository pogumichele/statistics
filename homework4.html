Homework 4: The Law of Large Numbers

What is the Law of Large Numbers (LLN)?

The Law of Large Numbers is a fundamental theorem in probability theory. In simple terms, it states that if you repeat an experiment (like tossing a coin) a very large number of times, the average of the results obtained (the "empirical frequency") will tend to get closer and closer to the expected value (the theoretical probability).

For a fair coin, the theoretical probability of getting "Heads" is 0.5 (or 50%). The LLN tells us that if we toss the coin thousands (or millions) of times, the percentage of "Heads" we get will be very, very close to 50%.

The Simulation: Coin Toss

This simulation does exactly that. For each "toss":

  • It generates a random number between 0.0 and 1.0 (using Math.random()).
  • If the number is < 0.5, we consider it "Tails" (0).
  • If the number is >= 0.5, we consider it "Heads" (1).

Chart 1: Random Walk (Cumulative Values)

Chart 1 Analysis

In the chart above, you will see two lines:

  • Blue Line (Cumulative Heads): This is the "random walk" of our results. It goes up by 1 every time "Heads" (1) comes out and stays flat when "Tails" (0) comes out.
  • Red Line (Expected Value 50%): This is theoretical perfection. It's a straight line showing where the blue line *should* be if exactly 50% of the tosses were "Heads".

As you can see, the blue line (reality) oscillates around the red line (theory). It might stray far at the beginning, but the more tosses you add, the more the blue line will be "forced" to follow the trend of the red line. The proportion (shown in the stats) will stabilize closer and closer to 50%.

Chart 2: Proportion Convergence (LLN)

Chart 2 Analysis (The Distribution)

This second chart is the true demonstration of the Law of Large Numbers. It shows the proportion (or frequency) of heads (calculated as Total Heads / Total Tosses) at each step.

  • Blue Line (Actual Proportion): At the beginning (few tosses), the proportion is very volatile (e.g., 0%, 100%, 50%, 33%...).
  • Red Line (Expected Value 50%): This is a flat line at 0.5.

As you can see, as the number of tosses (X-axis) increases, the blue line stops oscillating wildly and "stabilizes," converging more and more precisely towards the red line at 0.5 (50%).
